\section{Discussion and Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

\subsection{Flash Storage and Hardware Roots of Trust}
Solid-state drives and mobile flash memory (eMMC/UFS) utilize internal Flash Translation Layers (FTL) that perform out-of-place writes for wear-leveling, leaving stale physical pages readable until garbage collection~\cite{wei2011reliably}. VANISH resolves this physical constraint via Tier 2 Crypto-Shredding~\cite{diesburg2010survey, reardon2013sok}: by encrypting payloads with AES-256-GCM and maintaining Content Encryption Keys exclusively within hardware keystores (ARM TrustZone TEE or TPM 2.0 / Secure Element), key deletion at $t \ge t_{\text{exp}}$ renders remaining flash ciphertext computationally indecipherable. To counter clock rollback offline, time checkpoints are anchored to non-volatile monotonic hardware counters (eMMC/UFS RPMB or TPM 2.0 NV counters~\cite{tpm20specification}) coupled with Network Time Security (NTS)~\cite{rfc8915nts}.

\subsection{Deployment Scope, Limitations, and Future Work}
Because VANISH relies on kernel monitoring (\texttt{fanotify}), privileged alarm scheduling, and hardware rollback resistance (\texttt{Tag::ROLLBACK\_RESISTANCE}), it cannot operate as an unprivileged app on public app stores (e.g., Google Play). Instead, it targets platform integration: Enterprise MDM fleets in Device Owner mode, hardened OEM distributions (e.g., GrapheneOS, Samsung Knox), and upstream AOSP/systemd standardization. While VANISH prevents offline accumulation, it cannot prevent authorized recipients from photographing an active display (the analog hole). Future work includes transient framebuffers via confidential computing (ARM RME, Keystone) and post-quantum migration to NIST FIPS 203/204 algorithms (ML-KEM, ML-DSA).

\subsection{Conclusion}
We presented \textbf{VANISH}, an open protocol and OS architecture for cryptographically authenticated, self-expiring digital files. By coupling canonical CBOR metadata, Ed25519 signatures, and authenticated AES-256-GCM encryption with native operating system storage monitors, VANISH bridges the gap between ephemeral messaging sandboxes and permanent filesystem storage. Our reference implementation demonstrates strictly bounded framing ($140$\,bytes fixed, $320$--$429$\,bytes total), fast verification ($1.42$\,ms for $1$\,MB), and negligible idle battery drain ($<0.05\%$ over 24 hours).
