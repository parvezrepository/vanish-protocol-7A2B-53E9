\section{Discussion and Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

\subsection{Flash Memory and Hardware Roots of Trust}
On modern solid-state drives (SSDs) and mobile flash storage (eMMC and UFS), internal Flash Translation Layers (FTL) perform out-of-place writes for wear-leveling, leaving stale physical pages readable until garbage collection~\cite{wei2011reliably}. VANISH addresses this physical limit via Tier 2 Crypto-Shredding~\cite{diesburg2010survey, geambasu2011keymon}. By encrypting payloads with AES-256-GCM and maintaining Content Encryption Keys exclusively within hardware keystores (ARM TrustZone or TPM 2.0), key deletion at $t \ge t_{\text{exp}}$ renders remaining flash ciphertext mathematically indecipherable.

To defend against privileged local adversaries manipulating system clocks, deployments can anchor time checkpoints to non-volatile hardware monotonic counters—such as the Replay Protected Memory Block (RPMB) in mobile UFS storage or TPM 2.0 NV counters~\cite{tpm20specification}—coupled with authenticated network time (NTS~\cite{rfc8915nts} and Roughtime~\cite{roughtime2018}).

\subsection{Conclusion}
In this paper, we presented \textbf{VANISH}, an open, cross-platform protocol and operating system subsystem for cryptographically verifiable, self-expiring digital files. By integrating canonical CBOR metadata, Ed25519 digital signatures, and authenticated AES-256-GCM encryption with native operating system storage monitors, VANISH bridges the gap between closed messaging sandboxes and persistent filesystem storage.

Our reference implementation—comprising a desktop Linux daemon (\texttt{vanishd}) and an Android AOSP framework service (\texttt{VanishManagerService})—demonstrates that native file lifecycle management requires bounded framing overhead ($140$\,bytes fixed binary framing, $320$--$429$\,bytes total with manifest), verifies a $1$\,MB container in $\approx 1.38$\,ms, and executes on mobile devices with negligible incremental idle battery consumption ($<0.05\%$ over 24 hours). By formalizing threat models and operational tiers across cooperative and adversarial environments, VANISH provides a practical, open foundation for digital privacy and automated storage hygiene.
