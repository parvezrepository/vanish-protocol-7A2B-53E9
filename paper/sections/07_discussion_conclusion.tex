\section{Discussion and Limitations}
\label{sec:discussion}

\textbf{Flash Storage and Hardware Roots of Trust:} Solid-state drives and mobile flash memory (eMMC/UFS) utilize internal Flash Translation Layers (FTL) that perform out-of-place writes for wear-leveling, leaving stale physical pages readable until garbage collection~\cite{wei2011reliably}. VANISH resolves this physical constraint via Tier 2 Crypto-Shredding~\cite{diesburg2010survey, reardon2013sok}: by encrypting payloads with AES-256-GCM and maintaining Content Encryption Keys exclusively within hardware keystores (ARM TrustZone TEE or TPM 2.0 / Secure Element), key deletion at $t \ge t_{\text{exp}}$ renders remaining flash ciphertext computationally indecipherable. To counter clock rollback offline, time checkpoints are anchored to non-volatile monotonic hardware counters (eMMC/UFS RPMB or TPM 2.0 NV counters~\cite{tpm20specification}) coupled with Network Time Security (NTS)~\cite{rfc8915nts}.

\textbf{Deployment Scope and Limitations:} Because VANISH relies on kernel monitoring (\texttt{fanotify}), privileged alarm scheduling, and hardware rollback resistance (\texttt{Tag::ROLLBACK\_RESISTANCE}), it cannot operate as an unprivileged app on public app stores (e.g., Google Play). Instead, it targets platform integration: Enterprise MDM fleets in Device Owner mode, hardened OEM distributions (e.g., GrapheneOS, Samsung Knox), and upstream AOSP/systemd standardization. While VANISH prevents offline accumulation, it cannot prevent authorized recipients from photographing an active display (the analog hole). Future work includes transient framebuffers via confidential computing (ARM RME, Keystone) and post-quantum migration to NIST FIPS 203/204 algorithms (ML-KEM, ML-DSA).

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}
In this paper, we presented VANISH, a cross-platform, cryptographically authenticated protocol and OS-native enforcement subsystem for self-expiring digital files. By encapsulating arbitrary payloads into a self-describing \texttt{.vfile} container with Canonical CBOR manifests and Ed25519 signatures, VANISH enables portable, tamper-evident lifecycle policies that function independently of centralized cloud infrastructure. Our reference implementations on Linux and Android AOSP demonstrate that native OS interception, min-heap scheduling, and hardware-backed cryptographic erasure can be achieved with negligible overhead ($< 429$\,bytes per $1$\,MB file) and minimal latency ($1.42$\,ms verification). Furthermore, our empirical stress tests confirm robust protection against manifest tampering and clock rollback attacks. VANISH provides a practical, serverless foundation for automating data minimization compliance, offering a standardized pathway toward a future where digital files natively respect their own defined lifespans.
